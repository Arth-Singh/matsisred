Claim fails in general. Graph satisfies graphical front-door conditions, but decoder exposes missing overlap required for nonparametric identification.

Because \(d(M)=T\) almost surely, mediator distributions under \(T=0\) and \(T=1\) occupy disjoint measurable sets. Front-door adjustment requires
\[
P(y\mid do(t))
=\int P(dm\mid t)\sum_{t'}P(y\mid m,t')P(t').
\]
For each observed \(m\), conditional outcome distribution for \(t'\neq d(m)\) is unsupported by observations. Unlimited data cannot supply those missing combinations. Blocking confounding graphically does not resolve missing support.

Concrete counterexample: let \(U\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), \(T=U\), and \(M=T\). Consider two outcome mechanisms:

- Model A: \(Y=M\oplus U\), where \(\oplus\) denotes XOR.
- Model B: \(Y=M(1-U)\).

Both obey the stated graph and admit exact decoder \(d(m)=m\). Both produce identical observations: \(T=M=U\) and \(Y=0\). Yet their average causal effects differ:
\[
E[Y\mid do(T=1)]-E[Y\mid do(T=0)]
=
\begin{cases}
0 & \text{Model A},\\
1/2 & \text{Model B}.
\end{cases}
\]

Perfect intervention decoding therefore does not strengthen identification. It establishes observational separation between treatment groups’ representations; additional assumptions or suitable experimental information are needed for identification in general.
