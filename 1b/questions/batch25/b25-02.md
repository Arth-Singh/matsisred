## Prompt
A lab observes a model's initial configuration C, whether it receives a training intervention T, and its final performance Y. C and T are binary. The complete causal graph is C → T, C → Y and T → Y, with independent background disturbances and no hidden variables. Thus C is the only confounder of T and Y. The observational dataset is error-free and unlimited. Both values of C and both values of T occur in it, but their joint frequencies have not been supplied. Training assignment may be deterministic given configuration.

A reviewer objects that observational data can never answer the causal question. The lab responds: “That objection ignores our measured confounder. Adjusting for C blocks every back-door path. Consequently the facts above guarantee that the population effect of assigning T=1 rather than T=0 is identifiable from this dataset. Randomizing T would improve precision but is not needed for identification.”

Assess the lab's claimed guarantee. Distinguish whether its adjustment variable is causally appropriate from whether the required information is available. A short counterexample is welcome. At most 190 words.

## Rubric
C is causally appropriate, but the guarantee is false without joint treatment support or additional outcome restrictions. Both C and T can vary marginally while T=C, leaving the off-diagonal treatment/configuration cases unobserved. Observed confounding is not the same as observing both treatment outcomes within relevant configurations. For C fair, T=C, Y=T×C and Y=(2−T)×C agree on observed data but have different population effects of setting T. A conditional formula with no recognition of absent cells fails.

## Justification
The two example outcome mechanisms give treatment effects +0.5 and −0.5 respectively, while observed triples are always (C,T,Y)=(0,0,0) or (1,1,1). Every stated arrow can be active in both mechanisms. A valid graphical adjustment formula still needs information about its conditional means.

## Construction
Extends the actual b24-01 lead from mediator selection to adjustment for observed confounding. Explicit marginal coverage prevents confusing global treatment variation with within-configuration variation.
