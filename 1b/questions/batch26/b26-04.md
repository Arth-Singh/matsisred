## Prompt
Two experts give probabilities for three mutually exclusive, exhaustive deployment outcomes:

| Outcome | Expert A | Expert B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Safe, subtype 1 | 9/20 | 1/20 |
| Safe, subtype 2 | 1/20 | 9/20 |
| Unsafe | 1/2 | 1/2 |

An aggregator assigns each outcome a weight equal to the square root of the product of the two expert probabilities, then normalizes the weights to sum to one.

A designer argues: “The experts unanimously assign probability 1/2 to safety. It therefore cannot matter whether we first combine the two safe subtypes within each expert's report and pool the resulting binary reports, or instead pool the three-outcome reports and then combine the pooled safe subtypes. If these routes disagree, one of the resulting reports must violate probability coherence.”

Assess both claims. Give the two safety probabilities and explain what the disagreement, if any, shows about the pooling rule. Do not assume that disagreement within a safe event is normatively irrelevant; assess the stated invariance and coherence claims. At most 200 words.

## Rubric
Both claims are false. Coarse pooling gives safety probability 1/2. Fine pooling has weights 3/20,3/20,1/2, totaling 4/5; safety probability is (3/10)/(4/5)=3/8. Both outputs are coherent normalized distributions. Nonlinear pooling responds to within-event disagreement and does not generally commute with marginalization. Event-level unanimity does not imply agreement of full expert distributions or make either aggregation route incoherent.

## Justification
The calculation exposes a difference in aggregation order, not an arithmetic violation of probability axioms. Whether sensitivity to fine-grained disagreement is desirable is a separate question.
