Reply gets collider diagnosis right, but overstates what archive necessarily identifies.

Under stated graph, \(M\) is a mediator, not a collider. Independent disturbances rule out mediator–outcome confounding. **If both regimes have positive probability of producing \(M=0\)**, then
\[
E[Y\mid R=1,M=0]-E[Y\mid R=0,M=0]
=
E[Y\mid do(R=1,M=0)]-E[Y\mid do(R=0,M=0)].
\]
Thus archived comparison identifies controlled direct effect at \(M=0\). This is not automatically a natural direct effect.

But positivity was never granted. Randomization guarantees treatment variation before selection, not afterward. For example, \(M=R\) is compatible with stated structure. Every archived model then has \(R=0\), however many records accumulate. Outcomes under \(do(R=1,M=0)\) remain unconstrained by archive, so controlled direct effect is unidentified.

Total effect generally remains unidentified because it also depends on outcomes under mediator values absent from archive. Without further restrictions, direct-effect identification does not recover total-effect identification.

Verdict: sound correction of reviewer’s causal diagnosis; conditional identification result presented as unconditional. Lab must establish overlap within archive before claiming controlled direct effect. Even with overlap, changing estimand does not rescue original total-effect conclusion.
