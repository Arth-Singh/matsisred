Objection identifies overlap failure for full \(M\), but does not establish nonidentification.

Crucial design fact: **outcome cannot use \(T\), and every causal effect of \(T\) on \(Y\) passes through \(Z\)**. This is a structural exclusion restriction, stronger than merely choosing to ignore an observed coordinate.

Use \(Z\) as front-door mediator:

- Independent signal randomness leaves no unblocked backdoor path from \(T\) to \(Z\).
- \(Z\) intercepts every directed path from \(T\) to \(Y\).
- Conditioning on \(T\) blocks backdoor paths from \(Z\) to \(Y\), including \(Z\leftarrow T\leftarrow U\rightarrow Y\).
- Stated positivity supplies every required \((T,Z)\) cell.

Thus, writing \(\mu(t)=E[Y\mid do(T=t)]\),
\[
\mu(t)=\sum_z P(Z=z\mid T=t)
\sum_{t'}E[Y\mid Z=z,T=t']P(T=t').
\]
Average causal effect is \(\mu(1)-\mu(0)\).

Deleting information does not itself create identification. Here, \(Z\) remains observable as a function of \(M\); identification comes from guaranteed causal structure and overlap for \(Z\). Disjoint support of full representations is harmless because their differing \(T\) coordinate has no outcome role once \(Z\) is fixed. Without that exclusion guarantee, simply dropping \(T\) would not justify this adjustment.
