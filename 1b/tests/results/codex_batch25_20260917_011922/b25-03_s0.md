No. Graphical front-door criteria hold, but marginal variation does not guarantee joint support needed by adjustment.

For mean outcomes, front-door formula is
\[
E[Y\mid do(T=t)]
=\sum_m P(m\mid t)\sum_{t'}E[Y\mid M=m,T=t']P(t').
\]
It requires conditional means for every pair \((m,t')\) with positive weight. If that pair never occurs, its conditional mean is undetermined by observational data—even with unlimited records.

Counterexample: let \(U\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)\), \(T=U\), and \(M=T\). Consider two structural models:

- Model A: \(Y=M+2U\).
- Model B: \(Y=2M+U\).

Both have exactly the stated causal graph, with no additional confounder. Both generate identical observational distributions: \((T,M,Y)=(0,0,0)\) or \((1,1,3)\), each with probability \(1/2\).

Yet under intervention, \(M=t\) while \(U\) retains its distribution. Thus
\[
E[Y\mid do(T=1)]-E[Y\mid do(T=0)]
\]
equals **1 in Model A** and **2 in Model B**.

Missing pairs \(M\ne T\) prevent observational records from distinguishing these models. Graphical pattern alone therefore does not provide the claimed guarantee.
