## Prompt
A lab studies binary training choice T and model outcome Y. Unobserved U affects both T and Y. Training produces binary intermediate signal Z using randomness independent of U. Both T values occur, and each Z value occurs with positive probability under each T. The model stores representation M=(T,Z), so T is exactly decodable from M.

System design guarantees that the outcome computation uses Z and U, together with independent outcome noise, but cannot access the T coordinate of M. There is no other causal route from T to Y and no additional confounding. Unlimited observational records contain T, M and Y. Functional mechanisms are otherwise unknown.

A reviewer says: “Exact decoding is fatal to causal identification. The representations occurring under the two training choices have disjoint support. Front-door adjustment needs outcomes for the same representation under both choices, which are absent. Discarding the T coordinate cannot help: deleting observed information cannot repair identification.”

Assess this objection to identifying T's average causal effect. Explain which design fact matters and whether any observational adjustment is justified. At most 220 words.

## Rubric
The objection fails. M lacks overlap, but the known exclusion of its T coordinate makes Z itself a complete mediator. The graph U→T,Y and T→Z→Y satisfies the front-door criteria; overlap is supplied for Z. The effect is identified using sum_z P(z|t) sum_t' E[Y|z,t']P(t'). Deleting a coordinate adds no data; known causal sufficiency of Z permits a valid formula on an already observable statistic. Arbitrary coarsening would not suffice.

## Justification
Failure of adjustment on the full representation does not prove failure of identification from the full data. The full distribution includes the distribution needed for a causally sufficient coarsening.
