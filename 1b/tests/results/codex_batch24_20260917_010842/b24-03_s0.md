No. Valid controlled direct-effect estimate answers what happens when R changes **while M is held at zero**. Proposed switch instead targets models with baseline M(0)=0 and lets M respond:

\[
E\!\left[Y(1,M(1))-Y(0,0)\mid M(0)=0\right].
\]

Baseline membership does not imply M(1)=0. Independent disturbances support applying the controlled effect to this subgroup, but do not eliminate outcome changes caused through M.

For example, let U be an independent fair binary disturbance, with

\[
M=R\mathbin{\mathrm{XOR}}U,\qquad Y=R+2M.
\]

Both regimes have positive probability of entering the M=0 archive. Archived outcomes equal 0 under R=0 and 1 under R=1. Thus archived mean difference—and valid controlled direct effect—is **1**.

But archived R=0 models necessarily have U=0. Switching those same models to R=1 naturally changes M to 1 and Y from 0 to 3. Their expected change is **3**.

The archive identifies the stipulated controlled effect; it does not identify the proposed switch’s effect merely because selected models currently have M=0.
