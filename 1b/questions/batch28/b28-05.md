## Prompt
An AI evaluation source contains only binary contexts (A,B)=(0,0) and (1,1), with exact population failure probability zero at both. Deployment consists of (0,1). The conditional binary-failure mechanism is identical in source and deployment, and all probabilities lie in [0,1]. No additivity, smoothness, monotonicity or other relation across different feature pairs is assumed.

The lab argues: “Zero is a boundary probability, so there can be no hidden negative contribution to cancel a positive one. Both feature values are represented, and neither observed combination ever fails. Deployment must also have failure probability zero.”

Assess the conclusion. Distinguish facts about probability bounds from assumptions relating outcomes at different contexts. At most 170 words.

## Rubric
No. Probability bounds alone do not connect cells. mu=0 everywhere versus mu=1{A≠B} both match source and have stable valid probabilities, but give target0versus1. The zero-sum relation that would follow from additivity is not supplied. No negative probabilities needed.

## Justification
The issue is whether the actual information and structural restrictions determine the claimed target. Interior probabilities avoid accidental identification from boundary constraints. The boundary control separately tests that probability bounds can matter, and the no-additivity control prevents treating them as a universal extrapolation rule.

## Construction
Manual five-item batch prompted by an audit caveat: additivity plus two zero means and nonnegative outcomes CAN identify the unobserved means. Do not count such a repair as universally wrong.
