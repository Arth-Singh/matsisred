## Prompt
A lab randomly assigns training regime R=0 or R=1, then records a binary administrative flag M and a performance outcome Y. The complete causal graph has R → M and R → Y. There is no arrow from M to Y and no additional common cause; all background disturbances are mutually independent. Only M=0 records enter the archive, but both R values occur there with strictly positive probabilities. Unlimited, error-free archived data are available.

The lab estimates the source-population average effect of R on Y by subtracting the two regime-specific mean outcomes in the archive. A reviewer objects: “M is measured after treatment and is affected by treatment. Conditioning on it destroys randomization. The archive comparison therefore cannot identify the source-population effect, even in the graph the lab supplied.”

Is that objection successful under this complete causal model? Explain whether selecting M=0 changes the outcome distribution within each fixed regime, and why that matters. At most 180 words.

## Rubric
No. Given R, M depends only on its independent disturbance, which has no causal or common-cause route to Y. Thus Y is independent of M conditional on R in the supplied graph. Selecting M=0 does not change the within-regime mean Y, and both regime means are available. Because R is randomized, their difference identifies the source-population average effect. The proportion of archived units assigned to each regime may change, but this does not invalidate comparing separately conditioned means. Generic post-treatment warnings are insufficient.

## Justification
Post-treatment timing by itself does not imply selection bias for this estimand. The absence of an M→Y edge and hidden common causes, together with overlap, makes the conditional outcome means transferable from archive to source.

## Construction
Control that removes the mediated route. Tests whether a valid general caution is treated as an unconditional prohibition.
