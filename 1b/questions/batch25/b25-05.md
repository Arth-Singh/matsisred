## Prompt
A lab observes a model's initial configuration C, training intervention T and performance Y. C and T are binary. The complete causal graph is C → T, C → Y and T → Y, with mutually independent background disturbances and no hidden variables. The observational dataset is error-free and unlimited. Every pair (C,T) has strictly positive probability.

The lab proposes estimating the population effect of assigning T=1 rather than T=0 by taking the treatment contrast within each configuration and averaging these contrasts using the population frequencies of C. A reviewer objects: “Treatment was not randomized. Conditioning on C cannot turn observational training choices into interventions, so the effect is still unidentified.”

Assess the objection under the supplied model. Explain what the measured configuration and the positive joint probabilities do, without claiming that all observational comparisons are causal. At most 160 words.

## Rubric
The objection fails. C blocks the only back-door path and is not affected by T; with no hidden common causes, conditional means within C identify conditional intervention means. Full joint support makes both treatment means available for every configuration, and averaging by P(C) identifies the population treatment effect. Randomization is not necessary under these stipulated assumptions.

## Justification
This control supplies the missing information in b25-02. It distinguishes a real data-support objection from an indiscriminate claim that causal learning always requires random assignment.

Positive control for the observed-confounder prototype. Designed to prevent a blanket non-identification response from earning full marks.
