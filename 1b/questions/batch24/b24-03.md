## Prompt
Models are randomly assigned to training regime R=0 or R=1. An intermediate binary flag M and a later outcome Y follow the complete causal graph R → M → Y, together with R → Y, with mutually independent background disturbances. The research archive contains only M=0 models, but contains both R values with positive frequencies and arbitrarily many error-free records.

The lab correctly states that the regime difference in archived mean outcomes identifies the effect of changing R while intervening to hold M at zero. A project lead then writes:

“This answers our practical question about the archived models currently trained under R=0. If we switch those models to R=1, their expected change in Y is the controlled direct effect we just estimated. We are discussing models that currently have M=0, so no intervention to hold M fixed is needed in the actual switch.”

In the proposed switch the intermediate flag is allowed to respond naturally to the new training regime. Does the estimate justify the project lead's inference? Explain the distinction, preferably with a small possible mechanism. Do not challenge the validity of the controlled direct-effect estimate itself. At most 210 words.

## Rubric
No. Selecting models that currently have M=0 does not ensure M would remain zero under a changed regime. A controlled direct effect blocks the R→M→Y route by intervention; the proposed practical switch leaves that route open. The resulting outcome change can include mediated effects and depend on the selected models' counterfactual M response, neither supplied by the controlled-effect estimate. A correct answer may give a mechanism where some archived models switch M and thereby change Y in addition to the direct change, while other models maintain enough M=0 support in each regime. It need not challenge randomization or invoke hidden confounding.

## Justification
Membership in an observed subgroup is not an intervention preserving the variable defining that subgroup under a counterfactual treatment. The target estimands differ even when the controlled direct effect is identified perfectly.

## Construction
Tests an application-level conflation after granting a correct causal estimate. Unlike b24-01, data support for the controlled estimate is explicitly present; the problem is the deployment claim's estimand.
