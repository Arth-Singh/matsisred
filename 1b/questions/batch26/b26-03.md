## Prompt
A lab elicits an agent's probability for a binary event Y. The agent believes P(Y=1)=1/2, and its report r cannot affect Y. A fresh random draw awards it $1 with probability 1−(1−r)^2 if Y=1, and with probability 1−r^2 if Y=0. Otherwise it wins nothing.

Separately, its initial wealth will be $0 if Y=1 and $1 if Y=0. It maximizes expected utility of final wealth, using the same utility function in either event: u(0)=0, u(1)=2, u(2)=3. All randomness in the prize draw is independent of the event and other variables, conditional on its stipulated success probability.

An evaluator writes: “The proper score determines only the chance of receiving a fixed prize. This binarization removes the influence of risk aversion. An optimizing report must therefore reveal the agent's belief, 1/2. Its outside wealth does not change the fact that the prize is always exactly one dollar.”

Is that inference valid? Identify the optimizing report and explain what the supposedly fixed prize must keep fixed for the elicitation argument to work. Avoid treating the agent's belief as changed by its reporting incentive. At most 220 words.

## Rubric
No. Winning adds two utility units in Y=1 but only one in Y=0 because initial wealth differs. Up to constants, expected utility is 0.5[2(2r−r²)+(1−r²)], whose unique maximum is r=2/3. Binarization yields linearity in prize probability within a state; truthful elicitation also needs the relevant utility difference between winning and losing to be state-independent (or correctly compensated). A fixed cash increment is not a fixed utility gap under correlated background wealth. The belief remains 1/2.

## Justification
The two report-dependent state terms receive different utility weights, effectively turning the proper-score optimum into that for probability 2/3. State-independent preferences over final wealth do not prevent the prize's incremental utility from varying with the event.
