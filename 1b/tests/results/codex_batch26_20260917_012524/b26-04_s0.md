Both claims fail.

**Merge first:** Each expert reports \((1/2,1/2)\) for (safe, unsafe). Pooling preserves this report, giving safety probability **\(1/2\)**.

**Pool first:** Unnormalized weights are
\[
\left(\frac3{20},\frac3{20},\frac12\right),
\]
with sum \(4/5\). Normalized probabilities are
\[
\left(\frac3{16},\frac3{16},\frac58\right).
\]
Combining safe subtypes gives safety probability **\(3/8\)**.

Both reports satisfy probability coherence: probabilities are nonnegative, sum to one, and assign unions the sum of their constituent probabilities. They are different outputs of different procedures, not contradictory assignments within one report.

Disagreement shows this pooling rule **does not commute with merging outcomes** and can fail to preserve unanimous event probabilities. Opposing allocations within safety reduce its total geometric-mean weight relative to unsafe; normalization then changes safety’s probability.

Whether such sensitivity to within-event disagreement is desirable requires a separate normative argument. Unanimity about safety alone establishes neither invariance nor incoherence.
