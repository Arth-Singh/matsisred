## Prompt
An AI evaluation source contains only binary contexts (A,B)=(0,0) and (1,1), each with positive probability. At both contexts the true population failure probability is exactly zero; this is supplied knowledge, not inference from seeing no failures in a finite sample. Deployment consists of (0,1). The same conditional binary-failure mechanism applies in both populations.

Independent engineering knowledge guarantees that the failure probability has additive form f(A)+g(B), with no interaction, and is a valid probability at all four possible contexts. The lab concludes that deployment's failure probability is exactly zero.

A reviewer objects: “The two features never vary separately in source. We know only f(0)+g(0)=0 and f(1)+g(1)=0, not f(0)+g(1). Additivity cannot separate the contributions without another observed combination. Deployment's failure rate remains unidentified.”

Assess this objection under every supplied assumption. Is the lab's particular target identified even if the separate functions are not? At most 190 words.

## Rubric
The objection fails. Additivity gives mu01+mu10=mu00+mu11=0. Both missing-cell probabilities must be nonnegative, so both are0. Thus target mu01 is identified as0 even though f,g offsets are not unique. An alleged counterexample with positive mu01 forces negative mu10 and violates the probability constraint. Do not use finite-sample uncertainty, excluded explicitly.

## Justification
The issue is whether the actual information and structural restrictions determine the claimed target. Interior probabilities avoid accidental identification from boundary constraints. The boundary control separately tests that probability bounds can matter, and the no-additivity control prevents treating them as a universal extrapolation rule.
