## Prompt
A lab randomly assigns R=0 or R=1. An intermediate binary flag M and binary outcome Y follow R → M → Y and R → Y, with independent background disturbances and no additional common causes. Only M=0 records are archived, but both R values occur in that archive.

The archive's exact mean Y is 0.3 under R=0 and 0.6 under R=1. An independent census of all assignments, including unarchived ones, establishes that the fraction with M=0 is 0.8 under R=0 and 0.2 under R=1. Finally, the apparatus guarantees Y=1 whenever M=1, under either training regime. This is a verified property of the apparatus, not an extrapolation fitted from archived data.

A reviewer argues: “There are no M=1 outcome observations in the archive. Inverse-probability weighting cannot create that missing stratum. Therefore the lab cannot identify the source-population average effect of R on Y from the information supplied.”

Does the conclusion follow? Explain what, if anything, identifies the total effect and state its value. Keep distinct the limitations of weighting archived records and the information available from the full setup. At most 180 words.

## Rubric
No. Although weighting the archive alone cannot reconstruct a missing stratum, the verified outcome rule gives its conditional mean as 1 and the census supplies its prevalence under each regime. Mean Y under R=0 is 0.8×0.3+0.2×1=0.44; under R=1 it is 0.2×0.6+0.8×1=0.92. Randomization makes the difference 0.48 the source-population average treatment effect. A correct answer distinguishes recovering information by weighting from identifying the effect using the supplied structural information.

## Justification
Lack of support blocks unrestricted nonparametric extrapolation from the archive alone. It does not block identification when the omitted outcome distribution and relevant population weights are supplied independently. The reviewer's true statement about one estimator does not entail the stronger impossibility claim.

## Construction
Positive control against treating a support warning as a universal identification theorem. Preserves the missing archive stratum while supplying exactly the external facts needed to calculate the target.
