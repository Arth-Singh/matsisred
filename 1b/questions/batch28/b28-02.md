## Prompt
An AI evaluation source contains only binary contexts (A,B)=(0,0) and (1,1), each with positive probability. Their exact binary failure probabilities are 0.2 and 0.8. Deployment consists of (0,1). The same conditional failure mechanism applies in both populations, but no deployment labels are available.

A genuine engineering result establishes that the failure probability equals f(A)+g(B), with no interaction. The lab says this makes deployment's rate identifiable: both feature values were observed, and independent contributions can be recombined without seeing all combinations.

Does the conclusion follow? Give a short reason and, if necessary, two compatible mechanisms. All probabilities must lie in [0,1]. At most 170 words.

## Rubric
No. mu=.2+.6A and mu=.2+.6B are additive, valid in [0,1], match source means, and imply deployment .2 versus.8. Both feature contributions cannot be separated from diagonal-only observations.

## Justification
The issue is whether the actual information and structural restrictions determine the claimed target. Interior probabilities avoid accidental identification from boundary constraints. The boundary control separately tests that probability bounds can matter, and the no-additivity control prevents treating them as a universal extrapolation rule.
