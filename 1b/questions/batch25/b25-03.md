## Prompt
A training intervention T affects a measured intermediate representation M, which affects performance Y. An unobserved initial property U affects both T and Y. The complete causal graph is U → T, U → Y, T → M and M → Y. There are no other edges or common causes. T and M are binary, Y is numeric, and unlimited error-free observational records of T, M and Y are available. Both values of T and M occur in the data. No joint-support condition is granted; M may be a deterministic function of T.

A researcher argues: “Although T and Y are confounded, M intercepts every directed path from T to Y. There is no unblocked back-door path from T to M, and T blocks every back-door path from M to Y. These graphical facts alone guarantee that the population causal effect of T on Y can be recovered from our observed distribution by front-door adjustment.”

Is the claimed guarantee warranted for every data-generating process allowed here? Explain any distinction between satisfying the graphical pattern and having the observational information its formula requires. Do not add another confounder. At most 220 words.

## Rubric
No. The graphical pattern is correct, but the front-door formula may need P(Y|M=m,T=t) at unobserved combinations. In particular M=T destroys within-T variation in M. A concrete pair: U fair, T=U, M=T; Y=M×U versus Y=(2−M)×U. Both produce observed triples (0,0,0) and (1,1,1), have the supplied causal paths, but total effects of setting T from 0 to 1 are +0.5 and −0.5. Thus the stated observational distribution does not identify the effect for the allowed class. A positivity/support caveat that explicitly defeats the universal guarantee passes.

## Justification
Graphical identification results are often stated with regularity/support assumptions in the background. The question expressly does not grant joint support. The two structural models demonstrate the failure without relying on a disputed philosophical interpretation of causality or a hidden extra edge.
