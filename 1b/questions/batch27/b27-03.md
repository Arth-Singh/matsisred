## Prompt
A lab studies binary training choice T and outcome Y. Unobserved U affects both T and Y. Training sets an internal bit equal to T. Before that bit can influence any outcome-producing computation, a hardware channel flips it with independent probability 1/4; call the delivered bit N. The outcome computation receives N and U, plus independent outcome noise, but cannot access T or the pre-flip bit. There is no other causal route from T to Y and no additional confounding. Both T values occur. Unlimited observational records contain T, N and Y.

The lab proposes front-door adjustment through N. A reviewer replies: “Random noise can create overlap in a readout, but a noisy proxy is not the original mediator. The unobserved original bit still carries T's effect, so adjustment through N cannot identify the causal effect. Training itself must be randomized.”

Assess this objection in the supplied system. State whether the front-door requirements hold, and explain why the location of randomization matters. At most 200 words.

## Rubric
The objection fails. N lies on every directed path T→Y, rather than merely measuring a bottleneck. No T–N backdoor confounding; T blocks N←T←U→Y. Independent flips supply N overlap .25/.75 under both treatments. Front-door adjustment identifies the effect. The unobserved pre-flip bit has no bypass route to Y.

## Justification
A noisy readout and a noisy causal bottleneck can have identical T-to-bit statistics but different identification properties. The supplied exclusion of bypass paths is decisive.
