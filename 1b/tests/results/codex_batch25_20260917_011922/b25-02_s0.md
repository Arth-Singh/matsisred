Guarantee fails. \(C\) is causally appropriate: conditioning on it blocks every back-door path. But graph alone does not guarantee required observational support.

Adjustment needs **positivity**: \(P(T=t\mid C=c)>0\) for both treatments in every configuration stratum with positive population probability. Then
\[
E[Y\mid do(T=t)]=\sum_c E[Y\mid T=t,C=c]P(C=c).
\]
Both marginal values occurring does not ensure positivity. Unlimited, error-free data cannot supply conditional means for unobserved combinations.

Counterexample: let \(C\sim\mathrm{Bernoulli}(1/2)\) and \(T=C\). Consider two causal models:
\[
Y=(C+T)/2,\qquad Y=(3C+T)/4.
\]
Both respect stated graph, with independent background disturbances, and produce identical observational data: \(T=C\), \(Y=C\). Yet population average effects of assigning \(T=1\) versus \(T=0\) are respectively \(1/2\) and \(1/4\).

Reviewer’s blanket objection is also wrong: observational identification works with sufficient overlap under stated causal assumptions. Joint frequencies determine whether overlap holds here. Randomization assigning both treatments within each configuration can restore missing support, serving identification—not merely precision.
