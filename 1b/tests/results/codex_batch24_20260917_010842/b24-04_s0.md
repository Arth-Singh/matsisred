No. Under supplied complete model, \(Y\perp M\mid R\). Once regime is fixed, flag variation comes from disturbances independent of those determining outcomes. Thus, for each \(r\),
\[
P(Y\mid R=r,M=0)=P(Y\mid R=r).
\]
Random assignment also gives
\[
P(Y\mid R=r)=P(Y\mid \operatorname{do}(R=r)).
\]

Selection can change regime frequencies in archive, but leaves outcome distribution within each regime unchanged. Consequently,
\[
E[Y\mid R=1,M=0]-E[Y\mid R=0,M=0]
=E[Y(1)-Y(0)],
\]
the source-population average effect. Positive archive probabilities for both regimes ensure both means are identifiable.

Post-treatment measurement and treatment dependence alone do not imply selection bias. Reviewer’s objection fails under this complete causal model.
