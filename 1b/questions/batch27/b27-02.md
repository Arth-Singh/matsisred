## Prompt
A lab studies the causal effect of binary training choice T on outcome Y. The complete original graph is U → T, U → Y, T → M and M → Y; U is unobserved. M intercepts every causal effect of T on Y, but its observed values determine T exactly. Both training choices occur.

To repair missing overlap, the lab replaces the archived representation with randomized readout N. A sensor produces N from M using fresh independent randomness, chosen so both binary N values occur with positive probability under each T. The model still computes Y using original M; the sensor is downstream instrumentation and does not change that computation. Unlimited, error-free records of T, N and Y are collected. No other functional restrictions are known.

A researcher says: “Our original mediator satisfied the front-door graphical conditions; its only problem was support. The sensor fixes support without adding confounding, so front-door adjustment using N identifies the training effect. Added randomness affects precision, which unlimited data removes.”

Assess this repair. Explain whether the graphical and support requirements are now jointly satisfied. At most 220 words.

## Rubric
No. N has overlap but does not intercept T→M→Y: it is a noisy measurement child of M, not the mediator used by Y. Counterexample U fair,T=U,M=T,N=M XOR E with independent P(E=1)=1/4. Y=M+2U versus Y=2M+U give identical T,N,Y distributions but treatment effects 1 and 2. Sensor randomization changes measurement, not the outcome-producing mechanism.

## Justification
Repairing support with a randomized surrogate can lose the causal condition that licensed adjustment. Unlimited data and known sensor noise do not restore it.
